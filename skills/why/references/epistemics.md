# Epistemics

Classify each material claim by what the repository record supports.

- **Direct.** A source explicitly states the reason. State it plainly and cite it.
- **Supported.** Several indirect sources converge. Name and cite the evidence chain.
- **Inferred.** The record permits a likely interpretation but does not state it. Hedge and explain the reasoning.
- **Speculative.** Several explanations fit thin evidence. Present them as competing hypotheses.
- **Unknown.** The searches did not answer the question. Say what was searched and what remains missing.

Use causal wording such as "because," "was designed to," or "the team decided" only for direct or strongly supported claims. Use "appears to," "likely," or "suggests" for inferences.

Do not rationalize the current design. A sensible design may have come from a different constraint, copied convention, or accident. Do not turn absence of evidence into evidence of absence.

Test the leading explanation against a plausible alternative. Ask what evidence would differ if the alternative were true, then search for that distinction. Treat a hypothesis supplied by the user the same way.

When sources disagree, show both accounts. Explain whether they can coexist without choosing the tidier story.

Before returning, verify that direct and supported claims have citations, inferred claims show their reasoning, speculation is labeled, and gaps name the searches that failed.
