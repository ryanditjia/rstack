# Global English

Keep "only" and "not" beside the words they modify. Repeat a noun when "it," "they," "this," or "which" could refer to more than one thing.

Break long noun strings into explicit relationships. Keep verbs in every clause. Retain articles and connecting words when they make the grammar unambiguous.

Make the grouping of "and" and "or" clear. Use "both...and," "either...or," or a separate sentence when needed. Repeat the article for distinct entities, such as "the client and the host."

Use periods instead of semicolons or prose dashes. Avoid slash alternatives, Latin abbreviations, idioms, and metaphors that depend on cultural knowledge. Write "one or both" when that is the actual choice. Do not form plurals with "(s)."

Use one name consistently for each object and action. Do not churn an unchanged sentence merely to vary its vocabulary. Preserve literal symbols, quoted material, and required syntax.

Source: John R. Kohl, The Global English Style Guide, SAS Press. Adapted from the sample chapter and Internet Archive material consulted 2026-07-18.
