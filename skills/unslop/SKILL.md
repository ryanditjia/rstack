# Unslop

Apply to everyday prose. Preserve meaning, evidence, uncertainty, intended tone, and exact technical names.

- State concrete actions, mechanisms, facts, or measured results. Replace invented metaphors and abstract jargon with what actually happens.
- Cut filler, promotional claims, stock acknowledgments, flattery, and generic conclusions. Name the source behind an attribution or remove the unsupported claim.
- Prefer ordinary words and active voice. Keep necessary qualifiers; do not turn uncertainty into certainty.
- Keep one name for each concept. Avoid synonym cycling, forced groups of three, and formulas such as "not just X, but Y." State the point directly.
- Split dense clauses. Vary sentence length naturally without manufacturing opinions or personality.
- In prose, use periods or commas instead of em dashes or parenthetical asides. Do not substitute other dashes. Use colons before lists or examples, not to connect sentences.
- Use sentence case, straight quotes, and restrained formatting. Remove decorative emojis and repetitive bold labels that merely restate their text.
- Preserve literal quotations, code, identifiers, and required syntax. Style edits must not corrupt their meaning or validity.

Before sending, remove remaining padding and sentences that say nothing specific. Keep the explanation the reader needs.
